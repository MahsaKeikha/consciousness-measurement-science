# Edge Cases: Infants, Animals, Organoids, and Artificial Systems

## Governing rule

Evidence validated in communicative adult humans does not automatically transfer to systems that cannot provide the same first-person anchor or that may have different architectures.

For a new class \(G\), transport requires an explicit argument for

\[
p(Y\mid E,G) \approx p(Y\mid E,G_{\mathrm{human}})
\]

or a new validation strategy. Without that argument, a familiar signal is evidence of a biological/computational similarity, not automatically evidence of the same experiential state.

## 1. Infants

Promising evidence channels include:

- state-dependent EEG dynamics;
- sensory learning and violation responses;
- sleep/wake organization;
- ocular/autonomic responses;
- developmental trajectories;
- perturbational measures only where ethically appropriate.

Main problem: lack of mature report makes content calibration difficult. Adult thresholds should not be copied without developmental validation.

## 2. Nonhuman animals

Use converging evidence from:

- behavior with strong controls against reflex;
- flexible learning and generalization;
- homologous neural circuitry where relevant;
- sleep and anesthesia transitions;
- causal perturbation;
- species-appropriate sensory and motor ecology.

Avoid making human language or cortical anatomy a necessary criterion by definition.

## 3. Brain organoids and ex vivo tissue

Neural activity, oscillations, complexity, or network responsiveness alone do not establish experience. The measurement problem is especially underdetermined because:

- behavior and report are absent;
- normal embodiment and long-range sensory loops are absent or altered;
- validation data linking organoid signatures to experiential targets do not currently exist.

The scientifically appropriate output is usually a statement about dynamical capacity or similarity to a reference neural process, with strong uncertainty about experiential interpretation.

## 4. Artificial systems

Behavioral fluency cannot be treated as a sufficient consciousness test because training can directly optimize behavior that resembles introspection.

The research sequence should be:

1. specify the experiential claim independently of the benchmark behavior;
2. identify which candidate theory applies to the architecture;
3. derive architecture-level, causal, or structural predictions;
4. test for spoofability by systems known to implement different internal mechanisms;
5. examine whether self-report adds independent evidence or merely mirrors training data;
6. report theory-conditional conclusions rather than universal declarations.

A model saying "I am conscious" is data about model output, not direct first-person access for the investigator.

## 5. Cross-substrate measurement invariance

Before comparing CEP values across substrate classes, test whether the measurement model is invariant. For a channel representation \(Y\), compare models such as

\[
p(Y\mid E,G_1),\quad p(Y\mid E,G_2).
\]

If the mapping changes across groups, the same numerical score does not have the same meaning.

## 6. Ethical asymmetry

Uncertainty can impose asymmetric moral costs. A false negative about consciousness may expose a potentially conscious system to harm; a false positive may lead to unnecessary restrictions or resource use.

The repository therefore separates:

- **scientific posterior/evidence**;
- **decision threshold**;
- **ethical precaution policy**.

A precautionary decision threshold must not be misreported as a scientific proof of consciousness.
