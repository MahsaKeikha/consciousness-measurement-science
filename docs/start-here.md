# Start Here

## The project in one sentence

Consciousness Measurement Science asks **what evidence would justify a scientific claim about another system's experience without defining consciousness by whichever signal we happen to measure**.

## Why this problem is difficult

Suppose a person says they see a red circle. We can record the report, reaction time, brain activity, eye movements, heart activity, and perhaps responses to stimulation. Those observations are real. But none of them should simply be renamed "consciousness."

The measurement problem is the relation between the private experiential target and the public evidence.

For another subject, science observes

\[
D=(R,B,N,P,A,I,C),
\]

where \(R\) is report, \(B\) is behavior, \(N\) is neural measurement, \(P\) is perturbational response, \(A\) is autonomic or ocular physiology, \(I\) is intervention, and \(C\) is context and confounds.

The experiential target \(E\) is not directly available to the investigator in the same way.

The research question is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{Given }D\text{ and assumptions }\mathcal A,
\text{ what can be identified, bounded, predicted, or falsified about }E?
}
\]

## Five different questions that should not be collapsed

When people say "measure consciousness," they may mean very different things.

### Presence

Is there evidence that any experience is occurring?

### Global state

What multidimensional state is the system in, including arousal, connectedness, complexity, stability, and cognitive availability?

### Content

What is being experienced?

### Phenomenal structure

How are experiences related to one another in similarity, ordering, discriminability, or other experiential dimensions?

### Capacity

Does the system have the causal or dynamical organization associated with supporting experience, even when a particular content cannot be observed?

A method validated for content is not automatically valid for presence. A method associated with capacity is not automatically evidence of a currently occurring experience.

## Why one biomarker is not enough

A single channel can fail for reasons unrelated to experience.

- A patient can be conscious but unable to move.
- A sleeping person can have vivid dreams while disconnected from the environment.
- An EEG feature can change because of medication, arousal, pathology, or hardware.
- A task fMRI result can be negative because the person did not hear, understand, remember, or sustain attention to the instruction.
- A physiological response can occur without conscious processing.

This is why the project treats consciousness measurement as a **multimodal inference problem with uncertainty**.

## The Consciousness Evidence Profile

The repository's proposed research instrument is the Consciousness Evidence Profile, or CEP.

A CEP records:

- the declared target;
- the context;
- each evidence channel;
- data quality and interpretability;
- uncertainty;
- validation domain;
- assumptions;
- the strongest allowed claim;
- claims that are explicitly forbidden by the evidence.

The CEP can return supporting, opposing, mixed/dissociated, or inconclusive evidence. It is designed so that missing or uninterpretable data do not silently become evidence for absence.

## Why report still matters

Report is imperfect, but for communicative participants it is one of the most important anchors for conscious content. The goal is not to replace subjective data with "objective" data. The goal is to understand and validate the relation between first-person and third-person evidence.

No-report paradigms are valuable because they help identify neural activity caused by reporting operations rather than experience itself. They do not make first-person evidence scientifically irrelevant.

## The structural arm

The phrase "fabric of consciousness" is scientifically ambiguous. Instead of assuming that consciousness is made of a particular substance, this project asks whether the **structure of experience** can be measured.

For example, a participant may judge that experience A is more similar to B than to C. Many such judgments can define a relational geometry. Neural population states can also define a geometry. The project asks whether a preregistered mapping between these structures predicts held-out experiential relations.

A successful structural mapping would be important. It would still be a correspondence result, not proof that neural geometry and phenomenology are identical.

## The claim ladder

The project uses M0-M7 to keep claims proportional to evidence.

- M0: condition difference;
- M1: held-out prediction;
- M2: generalization across participants or sites;
- M3: generalization across different consciousness manipulations;
- M4: survival of confound and causal tests;
- M5: multimodal identification or bounds on a latent experiential target;
- M6: held-out structural prediction from physical to phenomenal organization;
- M7: unique preregistered theory prediction survives while rivals fail.

Higher levels require more than stronger statistics. They require stronger designs.

## What the repository currently contains

The repository currently provides the measurement contract, formal framework, CEP schema, claim schema, assumption registry, failure-mode registry, preregistration template, Phase 1 protocol, experimental program, statistical validation plan, structural framework, theory falsification matrix, clinical boundary, ethics framework, edge-case standards, and auditable software scaffolds.

It does **not** yet provide a prospectively validated universal consciousness meter. That is an empirical objective, not something that can be completed by documentation alone.
