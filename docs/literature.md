# Literature Map

This is a curated research map rather than an exhaustive bibliography. Sources are grouped by the role they play in the measurement program.

**Literature status:** curated through September 2026 for the foundational framework. Every empirical project should perform its own updated systematic or targeted review before preregistration.

## Philosophical target and explanatory gap

- Chalmers DJ. *Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness*. Journal of Consciousness Studies. 1995;2(3):200-219. Introduces the distinction between functional or behavioral problems and the explanatory problem of why physical processing is accompanied by experience.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. *Consciousness* and *The Neuroscience of Consciousness*. Useful orientation for philosophical and neuroscientific distinctions.

Repository use: the hard problem is not treated as identical to the measurement problem. The project can improve measurement without claiming to solve the explanatory gap.

## Global states and multidimensional measurement

- Bayne T, Hohwy J, Owen AM. *Are There Levels of Consciousness?* Trends in Cognitive Sciences. 2016;20(6):405-413. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2016.03.009.

Repository use: motivates caution about compressing global states into one simple scalar level.

## Covert physiological measures

- Kronemer SI, Bandettini PA, Gonzalez-Castillo J. *Sleuthing subjectivity: a review of covert measures of consciousness*. Nature Reviews Neuroscience. 2025;26:476-496. doi:10.1038/s41583-025-00934-1.

Repository use: motivates systematic treatment of eye, skin, respiratory, cardiac, and other covert signals while preserving the distinction between conscious and unconscious processing.

## No-report methodology

- Tsuchiya N, Wilke M, Frassle S, Lamme VAF. *No-report paradigms: extracting the true neural correlates of consciousness*. Trends in Cognitive Sciences. 2015;19(12):757-770.

Repository use: motivates designs that separate neural activity related to experience from activity related to reporting operations. No-report methods complement rather than eliminate first-person evidence.

## Clinical disorders of consciousness

- Giacino JT et al. *Practice guideline update recommendations summary: Disorders of consciousness*. Neurology. 2018.
- Kondziella D et al. *European Academy of Neurology guideline on the diagnosis of coma and other disorders of consciousness*. European Journal of Neurology. 2020.
- Wannez S et al. *The repetition of behavioral assessments in diagnosis of disorders of consciousness*. Annals of Neurology. 2017;81:883-889.
- Bodien YG et al. *Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness*. New England Journal of Medicine. 2024;391:598-608. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2400645.

Repository use: supports serial standardized behavioral assessment, multimodal testing, explicit false-negative pathways, and caution against equating nonresponse with absence of experience.

The 2024 multicenter study reported task-based fMRI or EEG evidence of cognitive motor dissociation in 60 of 241 participants without observable command following and task-based responses in 43 of 112 participants with observable command following. The repository treats this as evidence that both behavioral and task-based neural channels have important sensitivity limitations.

## Electromagnetic observables and field-theory hypotheses

- He B, Sohrabpour A, Brown E, Liu Z. *Electrophysiological Source Imaging: A Noninvasive Window to Brain Dynamics*. Annual Review of Biomedical Engineering. 2018;20:171-196. doi:10.1146/annurev-bioeng-062117-120853. EEG and MEG are noninvasive electromagnetic measurements linked to large-scale neural population dynamics; source imaging addresses the forward/inverse mapping between measured fields and neural current sources.
- Yang Y, Luo S, Wang W, Gao X, Yao X, Wu T. *From bench to bedside: Overview of magnetoencephalography in basic principle, signal processing, source localization and clinical applications*. NeuroImage: Clinical. 2024;42:103608. doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2024.103608. Reviews MEG measurement of magnetic fields arising from neuronal activity and the acquisition, signal-processing, and source-localization pipeline.
- Bastos AM, Schoffelen JM. *A Tutorial Review of Functional Connectivity Analysis Methods and Their Interpretational Pitfalls*. Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience. 2016;9:175. doi:10.3389/fnsys.2015.00175. Details common-reference, signal-to-noise, volume-conduction or field-spread, common-input, and sample-size confounds in phase and connectivity analyses.
- Jones MW, Hunt T. *Electromagnetic-field theories of qualia: can they improve upon standard neuroscience?* Frontiers in Psychology. 2023;14:1015967. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1015967. Reviews multiple electromagnetic-field theories of consciousness and their open explanatory and empirical problems.
- Hunt T, Jones M, McFadden J, Delorme A, Hales CG, Ericson M, Schooler J. *Editorial: Electromagnetic field theories of consciousness: opportunities and obstacles*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience. Published March 1, 2024;17:1342634. doi:10.3389/fnhum.2023.1342634. Presents electromagnetic-field theories as an active research program and explicitly notes that central field-as-consciousness proposals remain hypotheses to be tested.

Repository use: Research III separates two propositions that should not be conflated. First, neural electromagnetic activity is physically measurable with EEG and MEG and can be analyzed as an evidence channel. Second, the stronger proposition that an electromagnetic field or a particular field organization constitutes consciousness is a theory-level hypothesis requiring discriminating experiments. V16-V20 therefore validate physical calculations, organization descriptors, non-identifiability, and confound behavior without treating field strength, power, phase concentration, entropy, or complexity as a consciousness score.

## Perturbational complexity

- Casali AG et al. *A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior*. Science Translational Medicine. 2013;5(198):198ra105.
- Casarotto S et al. Subsequent validation work on perturbational complexity across benchmark conscious and unconscious conditions and disorders of consciousness.

Repository use: perturbational methods are treated as important measures of causal-dynamical capacity. Empirical thresholds remain implementation- and validation-domain dependent rather than universal metaphysical boundaries.

## Theory discrimination and adversarial collaboration

- Melloni L et al. *Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness*. Nature. 2025;642:133-142. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1.

Repository use: methodological model for preregistered divergent predictions, theory-neutral coordination, multimodal data, and the possibility that data challenge important predictions of multiple theories at once.

## Structural consciousness science

- Kleiner J. *Towards a structural turn in consciousness science*. Consciousness and Cognition. 2024;119:103653. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2024.103653.
- Lee-Youngzie Z, Tsuchiya N, Robinson M, Dietz D, Monti MM. *Towards a Mathematical Structure of Global Phenomenal Consciousness*. Entropy. 2026;28(6):615. doi:10.3390/e28060615.

Repository use: motivates the structural arm that compares phenomenal relational organization with physical-neural relational organization. Category- and sheaf-theoretic proposals are treated as active frontier formalisms, not settled measurement theory.

## Statistical and measurement-science principles

The repository also draws on broader principles from psychometrics, diagnostic-test evaluation, causal inference, calibration, external validation, domain adaptation, partial identification, missing-data analysis, and reproducible open science.

These literatures are methodologically important because consciousness research has an unusually difficult reference-standard problem.

## Source-selection rules

The repository prioritizes sources that help distinguish:

- measurement from explanation;
- report from experience;
- correlation from causal mechanism;
- causal mechanism from ontological identity;
- state from content;
- capacity from current experience;
- behavioral responsiveness from covert cognition;
- theory-derived markers from theory-neutral benchmarks;
- prediction from calibration;
- internal performance from transportability;
- negative detector output from evidence of absence.

## Citation practice for future studies

Each empirical study should cite the primary source for:

- the target operationalization;
- the measurement channel;
- preprocessing choices when inherited from prior work;
- the statistical method;
- the reference standard;
- the theory proposition being tested;
- known failure modes.

A literature citation does not transfer validation automatically. If a method is moved to a new state, population, species, hardware platform, or clinical setting, the validation domain must be re-established or explicitly limited.
