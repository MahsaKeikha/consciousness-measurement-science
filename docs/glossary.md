# Glossary

This glossary fixes the meanings of recurring terms inside this repository. It is not intended to impose definitions on the consciousness literature as a whole.

## Experiential target

The aspect of experience the analysis is trying to infer or predict. In this repository the primary targets are presence, global state, content, phenomenal structure, and capacity.

## Presence

Evidence relevant to whether any experience is occurring under the declared scope. Presence is treated as a latent target, not as a synonym for wakefulness or responsiveness.

## Global state

A multidimensional description of the system across variables such as arousal, connectedness, complexity, availability, and stability. The repository avoids assuming that all global states lie on a single scalar ladder.

## Content

What is experienced, such as a percept, image, thought, pain, dream element, or other reportable experiential item.

## Phenomenal structure

Relations among experiences, including similarity, ordering, discriminability, neighborhood structure, and potentially higher-order topology.

## Capacity

The organization or causal-dynamical capability associated with supporting experience, whether or not a particular content is currently accessible to measurement.

## Report

A first-person communication about experience. Reports can be noisy because of memory, language, criterion, demand, deception, metacognitive noise, and motor constraints.

## No-report paradigm

An experimental design that reduces or removes explicit trial-by-trial reporting so neural or physiological signatures can be separated from report preparation and execution.

## Covert measure

A physiological or neural signal used to infer consciousness-related information without overt behavioral report.

## Perturbational measure

A measurement based on the system's response to an external intervention, such as TMS-EEG. Perturbational measures probe causal responsiveness rather than only spontaneous correlation.

## Consciousness Evidence Profile (CEP)

A target-specific structured record containing evidence-channel values, data quality, uncertainty, assumptions, validation domain, allowed claim, and forbidden claims.

## Validation domain

The population, context, task, state, hardware, and analysis conditions in which the interpretation of a measure has been established.

## Identification

A property of a statistical or causal model in which different values of the target imply distinguishable observable distributions under the model assumptions.

## Partial identification

A setting in which data and assumptions restrict the target to a set or interval but do not determine one unique value.

## Calibration

Agreement between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies for a defensible reference target. Calibration is distinct from ranking or discrimination.

## Transportability

The ability of a measurement relation to retain meaning and performance in new populations, sites, states, hardware, or other contexts.

## Measurement invariance

Evidence that the interpretation of a measurement construct remains comparable across relevant groups or conditions.

## Confound

A variable that can create or alter an observed relation between a candidate marker and the target without representing the target itself.

## Necessary condition

Within a declared scope, a condition whose removal eliminates the target. Necessity is always conditional on the tested domain and intervention quality.

## Sufficient condition

Within a declared scope, a condition whose presence or production is enough to produce the target. This is stronger than prediction or correlation.

## Enabling condition

A condition that permits the target but is not sufficient by itself.

## Correlate

A variable associated with the target. Correlation does not establish necessity, sufficiency, mechanism, or identity.

## Abstention

A deliberate model output indicating that evidence is insufficient or outside the validated domain. Abstention is a scientific feature, not a failure to decide.

## Inconclusive

An evidence state in which data quality, assay sensitivity, model support, or identifiability is insufficient for the requested claim.

## Negative evidence

Evidence that lowers support for a target under a validated bidirectional measurement model. A negative detector output is not automatically negative evidence.

## Phenomenal geometry

A relational representation of experiences constructed from similarity, discriminability, ordering, or other structured first-person measurements.

## Neural geometry

A relational representation of neural states, such as distances among population activity patterns or causal response patterns.

## Structural correspondence

A tested relation between phenomenal and physical-neural structures. Correspondence does not by itself imply ontological identity.

## Claim level M0-M7

The repository's evidence ladder from condition differences through held-out prediction, transport, causal validation, latent-target bounds, structural prediction, and theory discrimination.

## Theory-neutral benchmark

An experimental task or outcome chosen to evaluate measurement properties without defining success by one favored theory's internal construct.

## Adversarial collaboration

A design in which proponents of competing theories agree in advance on divergent predictions, methods, and interpretation rules.

## Hard problem

The explanatory question of why physical or functional processing is accompanied by subjective experience. The measurement problem addressed by this repository is related but not identical.
