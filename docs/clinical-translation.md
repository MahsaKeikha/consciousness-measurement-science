# Clinical Translation

## Status and scope

This document defines how the research program could be evaluated in disorders of consciousness (DoC). It is not a clinical protocol for individual decision-making and does not convert the repository into a medical device.

Behavioral examination remains foundational, but severe motor impairment, aphasia, fluctuating arousal, sensory deficits, medication, fatigue, and technical limitations can hide preserved cognition or awareness. Clinical translation therefore requires repeated, multimodal, uncertainty-aware assessment.

## Why multimodal assessment is necessary

A large multicenter study published in 2024 reported task-based fMRI or EEG evidence of cognitive motor dissociation in 60 of 241 participants, or 25%, who showed no observable response to commands. The same study also found task-based responses in only 43 of 112 participants, or 38%, who did show observable command following. These findings matter in both directions:

- behavior can miss preserved command-related brain responses;
- task-based brain measures can also be negative despite observable command following.

The correct lesson is not that one modality should replace another. It is that each channel has failure modes and that discordance itself is scientifically informative.

## Proposed clinical evidence stack

A research evaluation should progress in layers rather than jump directly to a classifier.

### Layer 1. Medical and confound review

Document factors that can alter responsiveness or neurophysiology, including:

- sedation and other medications;
- metabolic disturbance;
- seizures and epileptiform activity;
- hydrocephalus or intracranial pressure where relevant;
- sensory impairment;
- sleep disruption;
- pain;
- autonomic instability;
- recent procedures;
- time since injury;
- injury etiology.

A measurement should not be interpreted outside its validated physiological conditions without explicit caveats.

### Layer 2. Serial standardized behavioral assessment

Use CRS-R or another validated standardized instrument administered by trained personnel. Repeated assessments are important because responsiveness can fluctuate and single examinations can misclassify state.

Record:

- time of day;
- medication timing;
- arousal interventions;
- sensory accessibility;
- examiner;
- subscale-level observations;
- reasons an item could not be tested.

### Layer 3. Routine neurophysiology and sleep-wake characterization

Standard EEG can identify seizures, gross state organization, and sleep-wake features that influence interpretation. Quantitative features may be added as research measures but should not replace clinical EEG interpretation.

### Layer 4. Passive paradigms

Passive auditory, semantic, novelty, or sensory paradigms can be useful when active command following is impossible. Their interpretation should remain modest: evidence of processing is not automatically evidence of conscious experience.

### Layer 5. Active task EEG or fMRI

Motor imagery, spatial navigation, selective attention, or related paradigms may provide evidence of covert command following when feasible.

A negative result must consider:

- hearing or vision;
- instruction comprehension;
- working memory;
- sustained attention;
- fatigue;
- scanner tolerance;
- motion;
- number of usable trials;
- analysis sensitivity.

### Layer 6. Resting quantitative markers

Complexity, connectivity, spectral, network, and source-level measures may provide state information. Their contribution should be assessed prospectively against strong baselines and should preserve site and hardware uncertainty.

### Layer 7. Perturbational measures

TMS-EEG and related perturbational measures can assess the brain's capacity for differentiated and integrated responses. Any threshold must remain tied to the specific implementation, quality criteria, reference population, and context.

### Layer 8. PET and resting fMRI

Metabolic or network imaging can contribute in centers where clinically and scientifically justified. It should be integrated with the rest of the evidence rather than treated as a standalone truth source.

### Layer 9. Repeat testing and longitudinal profile

When fluctuation or technical failure is plausible, repeat testing should be planned in advance. The longitudinal trajectory can be more informative than a single session.

## CEP representation in DoC

For clinical-research use, the Consciousness Evidence Profile should include:

- behavioral evidence;
- task-based covert response evidence;
- passive neural evidence;
- resting-state evidence;
- perturbational evidence where available;
- physiological and sleep-wake context;
- acquisition quality;
- assumptions;
- allowed claim;
- forbidden claims;
- explicit discordance.

The profile should never average away a reliable positive channel merely because another modality is negative.

## Interpretation states

Recommended research outputs are:

- **supporting:** at least one reliable modality supports the declared target and competing explanations have been addressed;
- **opposing:** a validated bidirectional assay provides evidence against the target within its scope;
- **mixed/dissociated:** reliable channels disagree or reveal a meaningful dissociation;
- **inconclusive:** sensitivity, quality, domain match, or identifiability is insufficient.

"No evidence detected" and "evidence of absence" are not interchangeable.

## Validation design for clinical translation

A clinical validation study should report at minimum:

- prospective protocol and locked analysis;
- external-site validation;
- sensitivity and specificity when a defensible reference exists;
- positive and negative likelihood ratios;
- calibration;
- abstention rate;
- failure-to-acquire rate;
- failure-to-interpret rate;
- subgroup performance by etiology and chronicity;
- site and hardware effects;
- repeated-assessment reliability;
- discordance analysis;
- sensitivity to imperfect reference standards.

## Reference-standard problem

There is no perfect third-person gold standard for another person's experience. Behavioral diagnosis, task-based neuroimaging, resting markers, and perturbational measures each have limitations.

Clinical research should therefore use imperfect-reference methods or triangulation rather than pretending one modality is error-free ground truth.

## Communication of results

A clinical-research report should state:

1. what was observed;
2. the declared experiential or cognitive target;
3. what assumptions connect the observation to that target;
4. what alternative explanations remain;
5. what the result does not imply;
6. whether the finding has been replicated;
7. whether the method is clinically validated for this use.

## Safety boundary

This repository is not a medical device and must not be used by itself for:

- individual diagnosis;
- prognosis;
- treatment selection;
- withdrawal-of-care decisions;
- pain-management decisions;
- communication claims;
- legal capacity determinations.

Any future clinical use requires prospective validation, specialist interpretation, governance, regulatory review where applicable, and a clearly defined intended use.
