# Clinical Translation

## Current clinical reality

Behavioral examination remains foundational, but severe motor impairment and fluctuating responsiveness can hide preserved cognition and awareness.

Serial standardized assessment is preferred over a single bedside examination. Major guidelines recommend multimodal evaluation, including EEG and functional neuroimaging in appropriate patients.

The 2024 multicenter cognitive-motor-dissociation study demonstrated covert command following in a substantial fraction of patients with no observable command response, reinforcing the need for multimodal assessment.

## Proposed clinical evidence stack

1. confound control and medical stabilization;
2. serial CRS-R or equivalent validated standardized assessment;
3. routine EEG and sleep/wake characterization;
4. passive paradigms where active tasks are impossible;
5. task EEG/fMRI for motor-independent command following when feasible;
6. resting quantitative markers;
7. perturbational TMS-EEG measures in equipped centers;
8. PET/resting fMRI where clinically justified;
9. repeat testing when state fluctuation or technical failure is plausible.

## Interpretation rule

Classify the patient's evidence by the **highest validated level revealed by reliable modalities**, while retaining discordant results rather than averaging them away.

## Safety boundary

This repository is not a medical device. It must not be used for individual diagnosis, prognosis, withdrawal-of-care decisions, or treatment without prospective clinical validation, regulatory review, and specialist oversight.
