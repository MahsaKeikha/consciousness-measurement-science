# Research Questions

This file defines the scientific questions that organize the repository. Each question includes the target, the empirical burden, and what would count as progress. The project does not treat every question as equally answerable today.

## Primary question

**How can consciousness be measured scientifically when the experiential target is private and third-person science observes only evidence channels that may be correlated with, caused by, enabling, or downstream from experience?**

The project answers this by decomposing the problem into twelve research questions.

## Q1. What is the measurement target?

Possible targets include presence, global state, content, phenomenal structure, capacity, access, and reportability. The first five are the repository's primary taxonomy.

Progress requires:

- an operational definition of the target that is not circularly defined by the same signal being evaluated;
- a statement of what observations can anchor that target;
- explicit separation from neighboring constructs such as arousal, attention, memory, motor capacity, and report production.

Failure condition: the target changes after results are observed in order to preserve a preferred conclusion.

## Q2. Under what assumptions is the target identifiable?

For observed data \(D=(R,B,N,P,A,I,C)\), under what assumption set \(\mathcal A\) can the latent experiential target \(E\) be identified, bounded, or partially identified?

Progress requires:

- an explicit measurement model;
- a list of required assumptions;
- sensitivity analysis to violations of those assumptions;
- a declared output when point identification is impossible.

A valid answer may be an interval or non-identifiability result.

## Q3. Which conditions are necessary?

Which biological, computational, causal, or dynamical properties can be removed while preserving the declared experiential target, and which removals reliably eliminate it within a defined domain?

Progress requires:

- interventions or natural experiments that alter the candidate condition;
- controls for downstream behavioral or report failure;
- replication across more than one context where possible.

A correlate does not become necessary because it disappears during one unconscious condition.

## Q4. Which conditions are sufficient?

Can any candidate condition be shown sufficient for a declared experiential target rather than merely predictive inside a familiar biological system?

Progress requires:

- a prespecified target;
- a candidate intervention that produces the condition;
- evidence that alternative enabling pathways are not doing the explanatory work;
- a defensible criterion for recognizing the target.

This is expected to be harder than demonstrating prediction or necessity.

## Q5. What causal architecture matters?

Which recurrent, thalamocortical, broadcast, integration, effective-connectivity, complexity, or other dynamical properties change causally with the target?

Progress requires:

- causal perturbation or strong quasi-experimental designs;
- temporal predictions;
- distinction between prerequisite, mechanism, consequence, and report process;
- theory-specific predictions that can fail.

## Q6. Can phenomenal structure be measured reproducibly?

Can similarity, discriminability, ordering, and other first-person relations be used to construct a reliable phenomenal geometry or topology?

Progress requires:

- test-retest reliability;
- measurement invariance across sessions or individuals where claimed;
- held-out prediction of relational structure;
- clear treatment of language, memory, and task-demand confounds.

## Q7. Can physical structure predict phenomenal structure?

Does a preregistered mapping from neural or causal relational structure to phenomenal relational structure generalize to held-out contents, participants, and states?

Progress requires:

- a fixed structural metric or mapping family;
- out-of-sample prediction;
- comparison against null and simpler models;
- bounded distortion or another preregistered structural criterion.

A successful mapping supports correspondence, not ontological identity.

## Q8. Which measurements transport across states?

Which measurement relations survive waking, sleep, dreaming, anesthesia, dissociative states, severe brain injury, and other altered states?

Progress requires:

- mechanistically diverse state manipulations;
- held-out-state evaluation;
- calibration and not only rank discrimination;
- explicit reporting of transport loss.

A marker that works only in one state is a context-specific marker, not a general consciousness measure.

## Q9. When is negative evidence genuinely negative?

Under what conditions does a negative channel lower support for preserved experience rather than merely expose assay insensitivity, technical failure, or inability to engage the task?

Progress requires:

- independently characterized sensitivity;
- quality-control thresholds;
- validated false-negative behavior;
- explicit rules for uninterpretable measurements;
- repeat testing when fluctuation is plausible.

## Q10. What observations discriminate theories?

What preregistered observations would favor global workspace, integrated causal, recurrent, higher-order, dynamical, structural, fundamental, or quantum-specific accounts over relevant rivals?

Progress requires:

- divergent predictions before confirmatory data access;
- pass and fail regions;
- shared stimuli and analyses where possible;
- explicit auxiliary assumptions;
- publication of theory-challenging outcomes.

## Q11. What could the "fabric of consciousness" mean scientifically?

The phrase may refer to very different hypotheses:

- a physical substrate;
- a causal organization;
- a dynamical regime;
- an informational or relational structure;
- a fundamental property;
- a bridge law between physical and phenomenal structures.

The research question is not which phrase sounds most compelling. It is what unique measurable prediction follows from each interpretation.

Progress requires converting each interpretation into a model that can be distinguished from alternatives.

## Q12. Where are the limits of third-person measurement?

Which consciousness questions remain non-identifiable from third-person data without additional bridge principles?

Progress requires:

- formal impossibility or non-identifiability results where available;
- sensitivity analysis showing dependence on untestable assumptions;
- clear separation between empirical underdetermination and metaphysical conclusion.

An honest limit is a scientific result.

## Cross-cutting questions

Every project stage must also ask:

- What is the reference standard, and how imperfect is it?
- What is the strongest alternative explanation?
- What would falsify the current interpretation?
- What happens when evidence channels disagree?
- Does the result replicate across people, sites, hardware, and states?
- Is the model calibrated?
- Does the method abstain when data quality is insufficient?
- Which claim level M0-M7 is actually earned?
- What does the result explicitly not imply?
